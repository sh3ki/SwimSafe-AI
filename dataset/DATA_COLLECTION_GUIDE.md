## 1. Introduction

This guide provides comprehensive instructions for collecting high-quality video data for the SwimSafe AI drowning detection system. Follow these guidelines to ensure consistent, reliable, and safe data collection.

### Objectives
- Capture diverse swimming scenarios
- Document drowning behaviors safely
- Maintain consistent quality standards
- Ensure proper safety protocols

## 2. Safety Requirements

### ⚠️ MANDATORY SAFETY MEASURES

#### Personnel Requirements
- **Certified Lifeguard**: Must be on duty during ALL recordings
- **Safety Diver**: Required for drowning simulations
- **Medical Personnel**: On standby for emergencies
- **Coordinator**: To manage the recording session

#### Safety Equipment
- ✓ Life jackets readily available
- ✓ Rescue tubes and hooks
- ✓ First aid kit
- ✓ Emergency communication devices
- ✓ AED (Automated External Defibrillator)

#### Emergency Protocols
1. **Stop Signal**: Establish clear hand signals for immediate stop
2. **Rescue Plan**: Brief all participants on emergency procedures
3. **Communication**: Maintain constant visual contact
4. **Time Limits**: Limit underwater scenarios to safe durations

#### Participant Health Screening
- [ ] Medical clearance for all participants
- [ ] Swimming ability assessment
- [ ] No recent health issues
- [ ] Signed consent forms

## 3. Equipment Setup

### Camera Equipment

#### Primary Camera (Overhead View)
- **Model**: Recommended 4K action camera or security camera
- **Position**: Directly above pool, 45-60 degree angle
- **Height**: 3-5 meters above water surface
- **Settings**:
  - Resolution: 1920x1080 minimum (4K preferred)
  - Frame rate: 30 FPS (60 FPS for high-speed scenarios)
  - Exposure: Auto with manual override option
  - White balance: Auto

#### Secondary Camera (Side View)
- **Position**: Pool side at water level
- **Angle**: Parallel to water surface
- **Purpose**: Capture underwater behavior

#### Backup Camera
- **Position**: Opposite corner for different angle
- **Purpose**: Redundancy and multiple perspectives

### Recording Setup Checklist
- [ ] All cameras synchronized (use clapperboard or sync marker)
- [ ] Battery levels checked (minimum 80%)
- [ ] Memory cards formatted and tested
- [ ] Lighting adequate (minimum 500 lux)
- [ ] Test recording performed
- [ ] Backup storage ready

## 4. Recording Protocols

### Session Preparation

#### Before Recording
1. **Pool Inspection**
   - Water clarity: Must see bottom clearly
   - Temperature: Comfortable for participants (26-28°C)
   - Chemicals: Properly balanced
   - Debris: Remove any obstacles

2. **Participant Briefing**
   - Explain scenarios to be recorded
   - Review safety signals
   - Practice stop signals
   - Confirm comfort level

3. **Camera Setup**
   - Test all angles
   - Verify synchronization
   - Check focus and exposure
   - Record test clips

#### During Recording
1. **Start Procedure**
   - Announce scenario number
   - Show clapperboard or speak scenario ID
   - Count down 3-2-1
   - Begin recording

2. **Monitoring**
   - Lifeguard maintains constant watch
   - Camera operator monitors framing
   - Coordinator tracks time
   - Safety diver ready to intervene

3. **Stop Procedure**
   - Clear end signal
   - Verify participant safety
   - Note any issues or deviations
   - Brief rest period between takes

### Recording Schedule
- **Session Duration**: Maximum 2 hours
- **Break Frequency**: 15 minutes every 45 minutes
- **Scenarios per Session**: 10-15 clips
- **Participant Rotation**: Change every 30 minutes

## 5. Scenario Guidelines

### Class 0: Normal Swimming

#### Scenarios to Record
1. **Lap Swimming**
   - Freestyle strokes
   - Backstroke
   - Breaststroke
   - Duration: 30-60 seconds

2. **Recreational Swimming**
   - Casual swimming
   - Floating calmly
   - Light water play
   - Duration: 20-40 seconds

3. **Swimming Lessons**
   - Instructor present
   - Learning proper techniques
   - Controlled movements
   - Duration: 30-60 seconds

#### Key Features to Capture
- Regular breathing patterns
- Controlled arm movements
- Purposeful direction
- Head regularly above water

---

### Class 1: Active Drowning

#### ⚠️ CRITICAL: Only simulate with trained actors and safety personnel

#### Scenarios to Record
1. **Vertical Struggling**
   - Body vertical in water
   - Arms extended laterally
   - Little to no leg kick
   - Head tilted back
   - Duration: 10-20 seconds (SHORT DURATION ONLY)

2. **Gasping Pattern**
   - Head bobbing up and down
   - Mouth at water level
   - No verbal distress calls
   - Erratic arm movements
   - Duration: 10-15 seconds

#### Safety Notes
- **Maximum Duration**: 20 seconds per take
- **Immediate Assistance**: Safety diver within arm's reach
- **Clear Signals**: Stop immediately on signal
- **Recovery Time**: Minimum 5 minutes between takes

#### Key Features to Capture
- Vertical body position
- Lateral arm pressing
- No forward movement
- Frequent submersion
- Silent struggle (no yelling)

---

### Class 2: Passive Drowning

#### ⚠️ CRITICAL: Use flotation devices for safety during simulation

#### Scenarios to Record
1. **Face-Down Floating**
   - Motionless in water
   - Face submerged
   - No movement for duration
   - Use safety tether
   - Duration: 15-25 seconds

2. **Submerged Motionless**
   - Just below surface
   - No visible movement
   - Clearly unconscious simulation
   - Safety diver touching participant
   - Duration: 10-20 seconds

#### Safety Notes
- **Safety Tether**: Always attached to participant
- **Contact**: Safety diver maintains physical contact
- **Breathing Device**: Optional for longer takes
- **Immediate Retrieval**: On any signal or concern

#### Key Features to Capture
- Complete lack of movement
- Face-down or submerged position
- No self-rescue attempts
- Floating passively

---

### Class 3: Swimming Distress

#### Scenarios to Record
1. **Fatigue**
   - Irregular swimming patterns
   - Slowing down
   - Difficulty maintaining position
   - Still able to signal
   - Duration: 20-40 seconds

2. **Mild Struggling**
   - Choppy arm movements
   - Head frequently underwater
   - Able to move but struggling
   - May wave arms
   - Duration: 15-30 seconds

#### Key Features to Capture
- Irregular but purposeful movement
- Ability to signal for help
- Struggling but still swimming
- Visible distress signs

## 6. Quality Control

### Video Quality Checklist
- [ ] Resolution: Minimum 1920x1080
- [ ] Frame rate: Consistent 30 FPS
- [ ] Exposure: Person clearly visible
- [ ] Focus: Sharp image throughout
- [ ] Audio: Clear scenario announcement
- [ ] Duration: Adequate for classification
- [ ] No camera shake or blur

### Rejection Criteria
- Person not fully in frame
- Poor lighting (underexposed/overexposed)
- Water too murky
- Camera malfunction
- Safety protocol breach
- Unclear scenario execution

### Validation Process
1. **Immediate Review**
   - Check recording after each take
   - Verify quality meets standards
   - Confirm scenario accurately captured
   - Note any issues

2. **End-of-Day Review**
   - Review all footage
   - Catalog successful takes
   - Identify reshoots needed
   - Back up all data

## 7. Annotation Process

### Metadata to Record

#### For Each Video
```
Video ID: V###
Date: YYYY-MM-DD
Time: HH:MM
Pool Type: Indoor/Outdoor
Lighting: Natural/Artificial/Mixed
Weather: (if outdoor)
Water Conditions: Clear/Slightly_murky/Murky
Temperature: ##°C
Camera Angle: Overhead/Side/45_degree
Resolution: 1920x1080
FPS: 30
Participant ID: P###
Age Group: Child/Teen/Adult/Senior
Gender: M/F/Other
Swimming Ability: Beginner/Intermediate/Advanced
Scenario: [Description]
Class: 0/1/2/3
Start Frame: ###
End Frame: ###
Duration: ## seconds
Annotator: [Name]
Notes: [Any relevant observations]
```

### Annotation Tools
- **CVAT**: Computer Vision Annotation Tool
- **Labelbox**: Cloud-based annotation
- **VGG Image Annotator (VIA)**: For frame-level annotations
- **Label Studio**: Multi-format annotation

### Annotation Guidelines
1. **Bounding Boxes**
   - Tight box around person's body
   - Include head to feet when visible
   - Update per frame or every 5-10 frames

2. **keypoints**
   - Mark 17 body keypoints when visible
   - Use visibility flags (0=not visible, 1=occluded, 2=visible)

3. **Temporal Segments**
   - Mark exact start and end frames of behavior
   - Note transition points between classes

## 8. Data Management

### File Naming Convention
```
[PoolID]_[Class]_[Scenario]_[Date]_[Take].mp4

Examples:
POOL01_C0_Normal_Swimming_20260115_T01.mp4
POOL01_C1_Active_Drowning_20260115_T02.mp4
POOL02_C2_Passive_Drowning_20260116_T01.mp4
```

### Directory Structure
```
raw_footage/
├── 2026-01-15/
│   ├── POOL01_C0_Normal_Swimming_20260115_T01.mp4
│   ├── POOL01_C0_Normal_Swimming_20260115_T02.mp4
│   └── ...
├── 2026-01-16/
│   └── ...
processed/
├── videos/
│   ├── train/
│   ├── val/
│   └── test/
└── images/
    ├── train/
    ├── val/
    └── test/
```

### Backup Strategy
1. **Primary Storage**: Local SSD/HDD
2. **Backup 1**: External hard drive
3. **Backup 2**: Cloud storage (Google Drive, Dropbox)
4. **Archive**: Long-term storage on tape or cold storage

### Data Transfer Protocol
1. Verify file integrity after recording
2. Create checksum for each file
3. Transfer to primary storage
4. Verify transfer success
5. Create backups
6. Verify backup integrity
7. Update dataset registry
